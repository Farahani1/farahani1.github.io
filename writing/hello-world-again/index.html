After experiencing about 100 days of Internet Blackout (IB), my motivation for writing this post is to raise the following questions in your mind:

  • Should you be more cautious about your relationship with the internet?

  • What part of your being, as a human, is entangled with your daily digital experiences?

  • Is internet blackout only a problem for Iranians, while the rest of the world will always remain safe from it?

At the moment I am typing these words, I am not sure whether they will ever find their way to any possible audience in the future. Here, I want to share my experience of the internet blackout and the unexpected effects associated with it. Briefly, after around 400 hours, it started to become a form of mental torture: depression, psychological complications, and a sense of self-alienation began to appear. Keep in mind that I am not a social media addict; I usually check social media for only 5–10 minutes a day.

Putting It All Together

Our experience of the present moment is not tied only to the exact second in which we are living. In fact, our feelings and state of mind have a dynamic continuity, carrying the effects of our experiences from previous days, months, and even years — as a matter of fact, from the day of our birth. Moreover, our state of mind and our emotions are deeply influenced by thoughts developed through human language. In this regard, when one has no human communication, the mind itself begins to change.

The Role of the Internet in My Life

As someone who grew up in a relatively socially disadvantaged situation, I used to plan and navigate a path toward the future and success mostly alone. Regardless of the endless love, support, and satisfaction I have always received from my parents, pursuing higher education and practicing recent advances in engineering — especially AI — was not something my family had much idea about. Until I made my first salary or had my first Skype meeting with Europe and the U.S., they could not really believe that I had any valuable knowledge.

As a result of the above-mentioned paradox in my background, I became a man thirsty for knowing what I should know. I developed a strategy of consuming more and more information from a fantastic open world called the internet, and processing it in my mind. I used to read, listen, and watch things from the internet almost every day of my life. As time went by, this routine naturally evolved, so that the content I looked for was not limited only to my professional field — AI and machine learning engineering. I also became curious about topics such as philosophy, sociology, complex systems, political science, strategy, management, and so on.

Eventually, I became a man whose mindset, thoughts, and language were different from those of the people close to him. Fortunately, thanks to the thoughts I had developed during that time, I realized that trust and love are the principal languages I need in order to communicate with my beloved ones and friends. I could really enjoy communicating with them and being around them. However, because of the gaps I mentioned, only on rare occasions could I have an argument or conversation that allowed me to delve into my thoughts, beliefs, and the knowledge in my mind. My thirsty cognitive needs had always been satisfied through the internet.

My Experience of the Internet Blackout

During the first week, you are full of anger and frustration.

During the second week, depression and low motivation for everything begin to appear — even for the things you had always wished to have free time to do.

By the third week, you realize that your brain has become extremely foggy, to the point that you cannot even think clearly or make simple decisions.

During the fourth week, you absolutely realize that you are literally not the same person you knew before. The best way I can explain my feeling during those days is to say: “I felt I was living a plant life.”

After the fifth week, almost all the anger had disappeared. I cannot even say that I was feeling depressed anymore. It was a very, very strange feeling of indifference toward almost everything. Even though I was not sad, thoughts of suicide came to my mind again and again. What frightened me was that this was a new experience: thinking of suicide while not feeling any drama or rush of emotion, almost as if it were a rational evaluation of whether or not to make a decision. I must mention that I did not use any medication that could affect my feelings or perceptions. It was simply the consequence of the changes in my lifestyle.

These strange experiences and feelings made me think about them a lot. The only explanation that came to my mind was that all of this could perhaps be explained from the perspective of cognitive science. I will not go deeply into this, because it is not my field of specialty. I will only refer to several concepts in the appendix of this writing, which might help give an idea. Now, I really understand those who have experienced a “silence chamber,” children who grew up in isolation and behaved unlike ordinary humans, and what might happen to a human being if its operating system began to malfunction.

Possible Conclusion

I believe that our being is entangled with our experiences of the digital world far beyond the level we might imagine. Once that connection is cut off, crises begin to appear. Definitely, this differs from individual to individual, depending on what each person has been doing in the digital environment. Unfortunately, based on my observations and personal experiences during those days, those who consumed content mainly for entertainment seemed to be less damaged by this experience compared to those who satisfied their intellectual needs through the digital world.

I believe that this is exactly the opposite of the common sense of the general population — and perhaps even of academia — where people often assume that the main challenge of the digitalization of human life is the way social networks entertain and distract people.

I think that if, in some fictional doomsday scenario, or as a result of wars and the political motivations of world powers, the earth were one day to plunge into digital darkness, the rates of suicide and depression among those who had intellectual consumption from the digital world would be far higher than among those who used it mainly for entertainment. The digital world has enabled thinkers to find rare individuals like themselves across the world, and to satisfy their need for thought and communication. Once the digital world is cut off, their being is somehow cut off as well.

Therefore, I think it is perhaps my duty to warn that such groups of people should at least be aware of this dependency. I believe this dependency cannot truly be felt unless one experiences such a catastrophe, and it is also often misunderstood by the general public.

Appendix

Before I begin to describe what I faced during the internet blackout, I want to draw your attention to three different, yet not entirely unrelated, topics.

Silence Chamber

If you have no background information in cognitive science, you might not know how important sensory input is for the functioning of the human brain — and how, through that functioning, it shapes our being. A method of torture that has been studied and developed by psychologists and intelligence services is to put a person in a box in which all sensory inputs are cut off: no sound, no light, no smell, the temperature set exactly to body temperature, the most comfortable body position, and no significant touch from any surface.

Just imagine yourself in such a position. How long do you think it would take to lose your sense of time? What input or trigger would your mind have in order to start processing thoughts? When I imagine this, I guess one might even lose the sense of the self. In any case, studies have shown that almost all of us, sooner or later, would end up losing our mental stability in such a situation.

Now that we have discussed this, it might also be worth mentioning the noble-minded German idealist philosophers who argued about whether our thoughts are anything beyond the reflection of our experiences.

Children Raised in Isolation from Human Interaction

This is a topic that contains both shadows of conspiracy and elements of scientific study. Throughout history, there have been several examples of individuals who were found not to have been raised by any human parent, usually as a consequence of war or human crisis. Observations of these individuals showed that they had a very low level of intelligence. Apparently, in some respects, they behaved like animals — for example, showing signs of anger or fear when meeting new humans. Because of human rights concerns, the study and examination of this topic has been limited.

Human Language as Our Evolved Operating System

This is an idea discussed in an article published in The Economist by Yuval Noah Harari. Although the main topic of the article is the risk of AI controlling human civilization, in order to support his argument, Harari explains aspects of the functionality of human language in our civilization. As he mentions, language is a principal software that an individual inherits from society, and it shapes the way he or she thinks and knows the world.

Arrival — 2015

Arrival is a movie that tells the story of the arrival of strange entities on earth. Later, it is discovered that these entities are aliens who experience time differently. They give human beings a powerful gift so that humanity can save those aliens in the future. Surprisingly, this powerful gift is a new language, one more capable and complex than ours. This language is expected to enable human beings to think and communicate more efficiently.
